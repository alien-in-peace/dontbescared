The Real Timeline

13.8 billion years, no shortcuts

The actual, cross-checked timeline of the universe, our planet, and our understanding of both — built from independent lines of evidence: radiometric dating, ice cores, stellar spectroscopy, and the fossil and rock record.

~13.8 billion years ago

The Big Bang & first light

Space begins expanding from an extremely hot, dense state. The Cosmic Microwave Background — radiation from roughly 380,000 years later, when the universe first became transparent — is still directly detectable today and matches this model's predictions to extraordinary precision.

~13.6 billion years ago

First stars & nucleosynthesis

The first stars ignite from primordial hydrogen and helium. Nuclear fusion in their cores and supernova explosions forge every heavier element — carbon, oxygen, iron, gold — and scatter them into space. Every atom heavier than hydrogen in your body was made inside a star.

~4.6 billion years ago

The Solar System forms

A cloud of gas and dust, enriched by earlier generations of stars, collapses under gravity. The Sun ignites at the centre; the planets accrete from the surrounding disk, including Earth.

4.6–4.0 billion years ago — Hadean Eon

Molten Earth & the Moon-forming impact

Earth is molten and repeatedly bombarded. A Mars-sized body likely collides with early Earth, ejecting debris that coalesces into the Moon — a history confirmed by matching isotope ratios between Earth and Apollo-returned lunar samples.

4.0–2.5 billion years ago — Archean Eon

First oceans, first life

Earth's crust solidifies and oceans form. The oldest confirmed microfossils and chemical biosignatures date to this eon — life appears remarkably early in the planet's history.

2.5 billion–541 million years ago — Proterozoic Eon

Oxygen, and the first complex cells

Photosynthesizing microbes flood the atmosphere with oxygen (the "Great Oxidation Event"), permanently changing the planet's chemistry and enabling complex, oxygen-using life. Multicellular organisms appear late in this eon.

541 million years ago — today — Phanerozoic Eon

Visible life: the fossil record

The Cambrian Explosion, dinosaurs, five mass extinctions, and the rise of mammals — all dated independently through radiometric dating of surrounding rock layers and cross-checked against ice cores and sediment layers.

~240 BCE

Eratosthenes measures the Earth

Using nothing but shadow angles at two known locations, Eratosthenes calculates Earth's circumference to within a few percent of the modern value — over two thousand years before satellites existed. You can reproduce this exact method in Backyard Tests.

1543

Copernicus & the heliocentric model

Copernicus publishes a Sun-centred model of the solar system, later confirmed observationally by Galileo's telescope (moons orbiting Jupiter, phases of Venus) and mathematically by Kepler's laws of planetary motion.

1687

Newton's law of universal gravitation

Newton formalizes gravity as a force between any two masses — the same force pulling an apple down and holding the Moon in orbit — unifying terrestrial and celestial motion under one testable law.

1915

Einstein's general relativity

Einstein reframes gravity as the curvature of spacetime. The theory's predictions — from light bending around the Sun to the time dilation your phone's GPS corrects for every single day — have been confirmed repeatedly for over a century.

1920s

Hubble & the expanding universe

Edwin Hubble observes that distant galaxies are redshifted in proportion to their distance — direct evidence the universe is expanding, and the observational root of the Big Bang model.

1969

Apollo 11 Moon landing

Humans land on the Moon, leaving retroreflectors that laser stations on Earth still bounce beams off today to measure the Earth-Moon distance to millimetre precision — an experiment anyone can find published live data from.

2021–present

The James Webb Space Telescope

JWST returns infrared data on galaxies from the universe's early history, star-forming regions, and exoplanet atmospheres — raw data that independent researchers and amateurs worldwide download and reprocess themselves.
